# Problem Statement

## Background

The power distribution industry depends on critical grid infrastructure such as transformers, substations, feeders, and other electrical equipment. These assets operate continuously under changing electrical loads and environmental conditions. Changes in parameters such as voltage, current, load, temperature, frequency, and vibration can indicate developing faults or abnormal operating conditions.

When these conditions are not identified early, equipment failures can lead to unplanned power outages, service interruptions, equipment damage, emergency maintenance, and reduced grid reliability.

## The Problem

Grid operators need to identify potential equipment failures and power outages before they occur. However, continuously changing grid conditions generate large amounts of operational data, making it difficult to manually detect every abnormal pattern and determine which equipment requires immediate attention.

The specific challenge is to develop an intelligent system that can analyze grid and equipment data, detect abnormal operating behavior, predict potential power outages and equipment failures, and provide timely recommendations for preventive action.

## Who is Affected

The primary users affected by this problem are:

- **Power grid operators** responsible for monitoring grid conditions and maintaining reliable electricity supply.
- **Maintenance teams and field engineers** responsible for inspecting, repairing, and maintaining transformers, substations, feeders, and other grid assets.
- **Utility organizations** that need to reduce unexpected outages, improve equipment reliability, and make maintenance decisions based on operational data.
- **Electricity consumers** who are ultimately affected by unexpected outages and prolonged service interruptions.

## Why It Matters

Unexpected equipment failures and power outages can result in:

- Disruption of electricity services
- Damage to expensive grid equipment
- Emergency repair and maintenance costs
- Longer outage restoration times
- Reduced reliability of the power distribution network
- Difficulty prioritizing maintenance activities across multiple assets

Early identification of abnormal conditions can give operators more time to investigate, prioritize high-risk equipment, and take preventive action before a developing fault becomes a larger grid disruption.

The project therefore focuses on improving the ability to move from a primarily reactive maintenance approach toward a more predictive and proactive approach.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Traditional grid maintenance can rely heavily on scheduled inspections, fixed maintenance intervals, manual monitoring, and reactive response after failures occur. These approaches can identify known or scheduled maintenance needs, but they may not continuously account for changing operating conditions and emerging combinations of risk factors.

Manual monitoring can also make it difficult to process large volumes of sensor and operational data consistently and identify subtle patterns that may precede equipment failure or an outage.

There is therefore a need for an intelligent system that can continuously analyze available grid data, detect anomalies, estimate risk, explain the factors contributing to that risk, and provide actionable recommendations that help operators prioritize preventive action.
